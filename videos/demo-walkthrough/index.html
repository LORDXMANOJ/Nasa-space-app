On Mars, Earth's answer takes up to twenty-two minutes each way.
In solar conjunction, there is no answer for weeks.
So the check-up has to happen on board.
The app opens with one question: who are you?
Ines picks herself, and sees the readings she took on Earth before launch. That is her normal.
Next, today's readings. Her wearable has filled most of them.
She measures the rest, and rates her mood.
Her results light up the body.
Heart and skin turn red: a fever, with a fast heart rate.
The line drawing marks the same places with arrows.
Can it wait for Earth? This fever is safe for about an hour.
Earth's reply takes sixty-four minutes. So: act on board now.
The crew follows a short, drug-free checklist,
and the medical officer decides.
Then one tap sends a consult packet to the flight surgeon.
In solar conjunction, it simply waits in the queue.
